# Donate

`sfw` is free, MIT licensed, and maintained by one person: me. If it's caught something for you, or saved you from reading a dependency bump line by line, you can chip in here.


## Where

- **GitHub Sponsors**, monthly or one-time: [xkilldash9x](https://github.com/sponsors/xkilldash9x) or [BlackVectorOps](https://github.com/sponsors/BlackVectorOps). Either one reaches me.
- **Buy Me a Coffee**, one-off: [buymeacoffee.com/blackvectorops](https://buymeacoffee.com/blackvectorops)
- **Cash App**: [cash.app/$kylemcallister2123](https://cash.app/$kylemcallister2123)

The Sponsor button at the top of the repo has the same links.

Using `sfw` at work? An organization account can sponsor through GitHub Sponsors too, which keeps it off your own card.


## Where it goes

- Servers that run `sfw` against new releases of real Go modules. That's how false positives get found and fixed before they fail your CI.
- Time. Most of what `sfw` needs is someone sitting down to find and close the next bypass.


## What it doesn't buy

Donating doesn't get you priority, a feature, or a say in what `sfw` flags. Security fixes ship to everyone in the same release, the way [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) lays out, whether you've given anything or not.


## Can't donate?

That's fine. A good false positive report, or a private report of something `sfw` misses, helps just as much. So does telling someone who gates merges on Go code that it exists.

Thanks.
